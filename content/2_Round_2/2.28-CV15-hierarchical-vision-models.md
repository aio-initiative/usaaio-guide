# 2.28 · CV15 · Hierarchical vision models: Swin, ConvNeXt and compound scaling

> **Goal:** A useful backbone must move information across space without making high-resolution computation unaffordable.
> **Stage:** Round 2, Core contest depth.

## Overview

Divide a feature map into non-overlapping windows. Attention within each window is cheaper, but two tokens on opposite sides of a boundary cannot communicate. Shift the window partition in the next block and some of those tokens enter the same window.

This does not make the entire image connected after one shift. Connectivity grows across alternating blocks, just as a convolutional receptive field grows across layers. Track this path before claiming that a layer has global context.

## Hierarchical Vision Architectures

### Window attention

Partition the feature map into non-overlapping $M\times M$ windows (typically $M=7$) and attend only within each. Cost becomes linear in $N$:

$$
\Omega(\text{W-MSA}) = 4ND^2 + 2M^2ND \quad\text{versus}\quad \Omega(\text{MSA}) = 4ND^2 + 2N^2D .
$$

The attention term goes from $N^2$ to $M^2N$: at $N = 3136$ (a $56\times56$ map) and $M=7$, that is $N^2 = 9.83\times10^6$ against $M^2N = 49(3136) = 153{,}664$, a $64\times$ reduction. The factor is exactly $N/M^2$, the number of windows.

### Shifted windows

Pure window attention never lets information cross a window boundary, so the model is 64 disconnected sub-models. Swin alternates: even layers use the standard partition, odd layers shift it by $\lfloor M/2\rfloor$ pixels. A patch at a boundary in one layer is at a window centre in the next, so information propagates across the whole image over pairs of layers.

The shift creates ragged windows at the edges. Swin handles them by **cyclic shifting** the feature map (a `torch.roll`) so all windows are full size, then masking the attention to prevent patches that are now adjacent but were originally on opposite edges from attending to each other. This keeps the batched computation regular, which is worth more than the alternative of padding.

The receptive-field arithmetic is the same as for stacked convolutions ([Sparse attention, sinks and eviction](/module/?id=sparse-attention-sinks-and-eviction)): each layer extends the reach by about one window, so a deep stack sees the whole image.

### Hierarchy

Patch merging concatenates $2\times2$ neighbouring patches and projects, halving resolution and doubling channels at each of four stages, exactly the CNN design rule of [CNN Fundamentals](/module/?id=convolutional-neural-networks). This gives multi-scale feature maps, which detection and segmentation heads need and plain ViT does not provide.

The stage resolutions for a $224\times224$ input with patch size 4 are $56\times56 \to 28\times28 \to 14\times14 \to 7\times7$, matching a ResNet's stride-4 through stride-32 pyramid, which is precisely why an FPN detection head can be attached unchanged.

**Relative position bias.** Swin adds a learned bias $B_{ij}$ indexed by relative position within the window, rather than absolute position embeddings, appropriate since the same window mechanism applies everywhere, and it means the model transfers across input sizes without interpolation.

### ConvNeXt

The important negative result. Take a ResNet-50, apply the training recipe and design choices that transformers use, and change nothing fundamental. The starting point is a stack of residual blocks, where the input is added back to the output so the layers only have to learn the difference:

![A residual block adds the input back to the output, so the layers only have to learn the difference](assets/module-media/web/cv4-residual.png)

*Diagram: [ResNet block](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:ResNet_block.svg) by Aston Zhang, Zachary C. Lipton, Mu Li, Alexander J. Smola, [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0).*

The changes, applied one at a time:

1. **Training recipe**: AdamW, 300 epochs (not 90), cosine schedule, Mixup/CutMix, RandAugment, stochastic depth, label smoothing. This alone takes ResNet-50 from 76.1% to **78.8%**.
2. **Stage ratio** (3,3,9,3) instead of (3,4,6,3), copying Swin's compute distribution.
3. **Patchify stem**: a $4\times4$ stride-4 convolution instead of $7\times7$ stride-2 plus max-pool.
4. **Depthwise convolution** (analogous to per-head attention mixing spatially) with an inverted bottleneck (narrow → wide → narrow), as in the transformer's MLP.
5. **Large kernels**: $7\times7$ depthwise, matching the window size.
6. **Micro-design**: GELU instead of ReLU, LayerNorm instead of BatchNorm, fewer activations and norms per block, and separate downsampling layers between stages.

Result: **82.0%** for ConvNeXt-T versus Swin-T's 81.3%, at comparable FLOPs.

**What this means.** A substantial part of the transformer advantage in vision was the training recipe and the design choices that came with it, not the attention mechanism. The honest reading is not "convolutions won" but "the architectural comparison was confounded, and once you control for the recipe the gap is small". Which in turn means: **choose based on your constraints, not on which is theoretically superior.**

### Compound scaling

**EfficientNet's observation.** Scaling depth, width, or resolution alone saturates. Scale all three together:

$$
\text{depth} = \alpha^\phi, \qquad \text{width} = \beta^\phi, \qquad \text{resolution} = \gamma^\phi,
$$
$$
\text{subject to}\quad \alpha\cdot\beta^2\cdot\gamma^2 \approx 2, \qquad \alpha,\beta,\gamma\ge1 .
$$

**Why that constraint.** FLOPs scale linearly with depth, quadratically with width (both input and output channels grow), and quadratically with resolution (both spatial dimensions). So total FLOPs scale as $\alpha\beta^2\gamma^2$, and fixing that product to 2 means each unit increase in $\phi$ **doubles** the FLOPs. The grid search found $\alpha = 1.2$, $\beta=1.1$, $\gamma=1.15$. The check confirms it: $1.2(1.21)(1.3225) = 1.92 \approx 2$ ✓.

**Why balance matters.** Deeper without wider means each layer is too narrow to carry the features depth is building. Higher resolution without more depth means the receptive field no longer covers the image. Wider without deeper means capacity with no compositional structure. The three are coupled, and scaling one alone hits the limit imposed by the others.

### Choosing

| Situation | Choose | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| < 10k images, training from scratch | CNN (ResNet, ConvNeXt) | the convolutional prior substitutes for data |
| Any size, pretrained available | **fine-tune a pretrained model** | almost always beats training from scratch |
| Large-scale pretraining (>100M) | ViT | flexibility pays once data is abundant |
| Dense prediction (detection, segmentation) | Swin, ConvNeXt, or ViT+FPN | multi-scale features are required |
| Edge deployment | MobileNet, EfficientNet-Lite | FLOPs and latency are the constraint |
| Frozen features | DINOv2 | strongest linear-probe features available |
| Multimodal | CLIP ViT | shared text–image space |


### From Scratch

```python
import numpy as np


def window_partition(x, M):
    """(H, W, C) -> (num_windows, M*M, C). Attention runs INSIDE each window."""
    H, W, C = x.shape
    x = x.reshape(H // M, M, W // M, M, C).transpose(0, 2, 1, 3, 4)
    return x.reshape(-1, M * M, C)


def window_reverse(windows, M, H, W):
    C = windows.shape[-1]
    x = windows.reshape(H // M, W // M, M, M, C).transpose(0, 2, 1, 3, 4)
    return x.reshape(H, W, C)


def cyclic_shift(x, shift):
    """torch.roll: makes every shifted window full size, so the batched
    computation stays regular. Masking then prevents wrapped-around patches
    from attending to each other."""
    return np.roll(x, shift=(-shift, -shift), axis=(0, 1))


def shift_attention_mask(H, W, M, shift):
    """Patches that are adjacent only because of the roll must not attend."""
    img = np.zeros((H, W), dtype=int)
    cnt = 0
    for h in (slice(0, -M), slice(-M, -shift), slice(-shift, None)):
        for w in (slice(0, -M), slice(-M, -shift), slice(-shift, None)):
            img[h, w] = cnt
            cnt += 1
    win = window_partition(img[..., None], M)[..., 0]     # (nW, M*M)
    return win[:, :, None] == win[:, None, :]             # True where allowed


def window_attention_cost(N, D, M):
    """4 N D^2 + 2 M^2 N D versus global's 4 N D^2 + 2 N^2 D. The attention
    term drops by exactly N / M^2 -- the number of windows."""
    return {"window": 4 * N * D * D + 2 * M * M * N * D,
            "global": 4 * N * D * D + 2 * N * N * D,
            "attention_reduction": N / (M * M)}


def patch_merge(x):
    """Concatenate 2x2 neighbours and project: resolution halves, channels
    double. The CNN pyramid rule, in a transformer."""
    H, W, C = x.shape
    x = x.reshape(H // 2, 2, W // 2, 2, C).transpose(0, 2, 1, 3, 4)
    return x.reshape(H // 2, W // 2, 4 * C)               # then a Linear(4C, 2C)


def swin_stage_resolutions(H, W, patch=4, stages=4):
    """56 -> 28 -> 14 -> 7 for a 224 input: exactly a ResNet's stride pyramid,
    which is why an FPN head attaches unchanged."""
    r = [(H // patch, W // patch)]
    for _ in range(stages - 1):
        r.append((r[-1][0] // 2, r[-1][1] // 2))
    return r


def relative_position_index(M):
    """Bias indexed by RELATIVE position within a window, so the same table
    applies to every window and transfers across input sizes."""
    coords = np.stack(np.meshgrid(np.arange(M), np.arange(M), indexing="ij")).reshape(2, -1)
    rel = coords[:, :, None] - coords[:, None, :]
    rel = rel.transpose(1, 2, 0) + (M - 1)
    return rel[..., 0] * (2 * M - 1) + rel[..., 1]


def compound_scaling(phi, alpha=1.2, beta=1.1, gamma=1.15):
    """alpha beta^2 gamma^2 ~ 2, so each unit of phi DOUBLES the FLOPs."""
    return {"depth": alpha ** phi, "width": beta ** phi, "resolution": gamma ** phi,
            "flops_multiplier": (alpha * beta ** 2 * gamma ** 2) ** phi}


CONVNEXT_ABLATION = [
    ("ResNet-50 baseline", 76.1),
    ("+ modern training recipe", 78.8),      # the recipe alone is worth 2.7 points
    ("+ stage ratio (3,3,9,3)", 79.4),
    ("+ patchify stem", 79.5),
    ("+ depthwise / inverted bottleneck", 80.5),
    ("+ 7x7 kernels", 80.6),
    ("+ micro design + separate downsampling", 82.0),
]
```

When comparing architectures, match the pretraining data *and* the training recipe. Most published vision comparisons differ in both, and ConvNeXt's ablation shows the recipe alone is worth more than the architectural difference being claimed.

### Worked Example

**Part A: the window saving.** A $56\times56$ feature map, so $N = 3136$, with $M = 7$ and $D = 96$.

$$
\text{global attention term: } 2N^2D = 2(3136^2)(96) = 1.89\times10^9 .
$$
$$
\text{window attention term: } 2M^2ND = 2(49)(3136)(96) = 2.95\times10^7 .
$$

A **64× reduction**, which is exactly $N/M^2 = 3136/49 = 64$, the number of windows. The projection term $4ND^2$ is unchanged at $1.16\times10^8$, so the layer goes from $2.00\times10^9$ to $1.45\times10^8$ FLOPs, a 14× total saving at this stage.

The saving is largest at the highest-resolution stage and shrinks as the pyramid coarsens: at stage 4 ($7\times7$, $N=49$) there is exactly one window and window attention *is* global attention.

**Part B: why the shift is not optional.** Without shifting, a $56\times56$ map with $M=7$ has 64 windows, and a patch in window 1 can never influence a patch in window 64 no matter how many layers are stacked: the model is 64 independent networks that happen to share weights.

With shifting by $\lfloor 7/2\rfloor = 3$: after layer 1 (unshifted) a patch has seen its own window, and after layer 2 (shifted) it has seen a window offset by 3, which overlaps up to four of the original windows (two along each axis). Its effective reach grows by roughly one window per layer, so a 12-layer stage spans about 12 windows, more than the 8 that tile the 56-patch map: it sees the whole image.


The same arithmetic as stacked convolutions, and the same conclusion: local operations plus depth give global reach.

**Part C: the cyclic shift, and the mask it needs.** Shifting the partition leaves ragged windows at the edges: strips of 3 pixels at the top and left and 4 pixels at the bottom and right, so some windows are $3\times7$ or $4\times7$ and the corners $3\times3$ or $4\times4$. Batching irregular windows is expensive.

Swin instead rolls the whole feature map by $(-3,-3)$, which makes every window full size again. But the roll wraps the top rows to the bottom, so a window near the bottom edge now contains patches from both the bottom and the top of the image, which are not spatially adjacent. The attention mask forbids those pairs, grouping patches by which original region they came from and allowing attention only within a group.

The result is one regular batched attention call per layer, with a precomputed mask, rather than a ragged loop. It is a systems decision that made the architecture practical.

**Part D: the pyramid, stage by stage.** $224\times224$ input, patch size 4:

| stage | resolution | channels | tokens |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | $56\times56$ | 96 | 3136 |
| 2 | $28\times28$ | 192 | 784 |
| 3 | $14\times14$ | 384 | 196 |
| 4 | $7\times7$ | 768 | 49 |

Compare a ResNet: stride 4, 8, 16, 32 with 256, 512, 1024, 2048 channels. Same spatial resolutions, same doubling rule. That correspondence is deliberate and is why every detection framework's FPN head accepts a Swin backbone with no modification, while a plain ViT, which produces one $14\times14$ map and nothing else, needs a purpose-built adapter.

**Part E: ConvNeXt's ablation, read as evidence.**

| change | top-1 | delta |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ResNet-50 baseline | 76.1 | n/a |
| modern training recipe | 78.8 | **+2.7** |
| stage ratio (3,3,9,3) | 79.4 | +0.6 |
| patchify stem | 79.5 | +0.1 |
| depthwise + inverted bottleneck | 80.5 | +1.0 |
| $7\times7$ kernels | 80.6 | +0.1 |
| micro design (fewer activations/norms, GELU, LayerNorm) + separate downsampling | 82.0 | +1.4 |

That last row bundles five separate changes, and they are not equal: substituting GELU for ReLU is worth exactly 0.0, cutting activation functions is worth +0.7 (80.6 to 81.3), removing normalization layers +0.1, LayerNorm for BatchNorm +0.1, and adding separate downsampling layers between stages +0.5. Most of the +1.4 is the sparser block and the separate downsampling layers, not the swap of activation function.

Swin-T scores 81.3 at comparable FLOPs, so the final ConvNeXt is 0.7 points *ahead* with no attention anywhere.

The first row is the one that matters: **the training recipe alone accounts for 2.7 of the 5.9 total points**, and the original ViT and Swin papers compared their new recipes against ResNet's old one. Nearly half the reported architectural advantage was a recipe difference.

**Part F: compound scaling, checked.** EfficientNet-B0 to B1 sets $\phi = 1$ with $\alpha=1.2$, $\beta=1.1$, $\gamma=1.15$:

$$
\text{depth} \times 1.2, \qquad \text{width}\times1.1, \qquad \text{resolution}\times1.15 .
$$
$$
\text{FLOPs} \times 1.2(1.1^2)(1.15^2) = 1.2(1.21)(1.3225) = 1.92 \approx 2 \ \checkmark
$$

Compare spending the same doubling on depth alone: $\alpha = 2$, giving twice the layers at the same width and resolution. EfficientNet's ablation reports that single-dimension scaling saturates around 80% top-1 while compound scaling continues past 84%, and the balanced version reaches accuracies the unbalanced one cannot at any budget.

## Intuition

Hierarchy changes the units of computation. At high resolution, local operations preserve location. After merging patches, each token represents a larger input region and global communication becomes cheaper. This is the same broad trade used by CNN pyramids, implemented with different mixing operations.
