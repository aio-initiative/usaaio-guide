> **Goal:** Temperature, top-k and top-p sampling.
> **Stage:** Round 2, Core contest depth.

## The problem

After training, the model gives a probability for every possible next token. To write text, one token has to be chosen and appended, and then the model runs again. There is no target any more, so the question is how to choose.

Both obvious answers fail:

- **Always the most likely token.** The text falls into loops, such as "the cat sat on the mat on the mat on the mat".
- **Any token, by its probability.** Each unlikely token is rare, but there are tens of thousands of them, so together they come up often. One odd token can derail everything after it.

An illustration of the two failures. Too little freedom repeats, and too much gives nonsense.

The model needs a way of choosing that keeps some variety and still avoids the unlikely tokens.

## Earlier approaches

### Greedy decoding

Taking the most probable token every time is simple, but tends to fall into repetitive loops (Holtzman et al., 2020). The running example below predicts "on" twice in a row.

### Beam search

Keeping several of the most probable partial texts works well for translation, where a sentence has roughly one right answer, but for open-ended writing it produces bland, repetitive text.

### Sampling from the full distribution

Drawing at random by probability adds variety, but the many unlikely tokens together get picked often enough to derail the text: 49,000 words with a 0.001% chance each still add up to a 49% chance of picking one of them.

## Temperature, top-k and top-p

**Temperature.** The **temperature** $\tau$ divides every logit before the softmax:

$$
p_i = \frac{e^{z_i / \tau}}{e^{z_1 / \tau} + e^{z_2 / \tau} + \dots}.
$$

Below 1 it makes the top tokens more likely, and above 1 it spreads the probability out. For example, the two highest logits after "the cat sat" are 3.197 for "on" and 1.530 for "sat", a gap of 1.667. At temperature 1, "on" is $e^{1.667} = 5.3$ times as likely as "sat". At temperature 0.5 the gap doubles to 3.334 and "on" is 28 times as likely. At temperature 2 the gap halves to 0.834 and "on" is only 2.3 times as likely.

<Figure src="assets/module-media/transformer-temperature.svg" alt="Three bar charts of the probabilities of the 8 tokens after the cat sat, at temperatures 0.5, 1 and 2. At 0.5 the bar for on is 0.957 and the rest are close to 0. At 1 it is 0.741, with sat and dog visible. At 2 it is 0.431 and every token has a visible bar.">
The same logits at three temperatures. A low temperature concentrates the probability on "on", and a high one spreads it out.
</Figure>

**Top-k.** **Top-k** keeps only the $k$ most likely tokens and samples among them. With $k = 3$ after "the cat sat", only "on", "sat" and "dog" can be chosen.

**Top-p.** **Top-p** keeps the smallest set of tokens whose probabilities add up to at least $p$, such as 0.9. The set grows when the model is unsure and shrinks when it is confident: at temperature 0.5, "on" alone has 0.957, so it is the whole set.

**Stopping.** Generation stops at the end-of-text token or at a length limit. To avoid recomputing earlier tokens at every step, models keep their keys and values in a **KV cache**, covered in [Efficient Transformers: 2. KV cache](4.9-TF27-efficient-kv-cache.md).

## Running example

### Temperature

The logits for the token after "the cat sat" are the last row of Step 12. The probabilities at three temperatures, from most to least likely:

| Token | Temperature 1 | Temperature 0.5 | Temperature 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| on | 0.741 | 0.957 | 0.431 |
| sat | 0.140 | 0.034 | 0.187 |
| dog | 0.066 | 0.008 | 0.128 |
| the | 0.016 | 0.000 | 0.063 |
| ran | 0.014 | 0.000 | 0.059 |
| cat | 0.011 | 0.000 | 0.052 |
| mat | 0.007 | 0.000 | 0.041 |
| `<end>` | 0.006 | 0.000 | 0.038 |

### Top-k and top-p

The strategies, applied to these probabilities:

| Strategy | Candidates | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Greedy | on | on |
| Top 3, temperature 1 | on, sat, dog | Sample with probabilities 0.783, 0.148, 0.070 |
| Top-p 0.9, temperature 1 | on, sat, dog (cumulative 0.741, 0.881, 0.947) | Same set as top-3 |
| Top-p 0.9, temperature 0.5 | on (0.957 alone exceeds 0.9) | on |

For the top 3, the kept probabilities add up to 0.947, and dividing each by 0.947 gives 0.783, 0.148 and 0.070.

### Generating again

Appending "on" and running the model again predicts "on" a second time, with probability 0.736. The weights were chosen for one prediction only, so greedy decoding repeats itself, the loop Holtzman et al. describe. A trained model would continue with something like "the mat", as in the diagram in Step 0.

```python
import numpy as np

vocab = ["<end>", "the", "cat", "sat", "on", "mat", "dog", "ran"]
z = np.array([-1.667, -0.639, -1.028, 1.530, 3.197, -1.530, 0.776, -0.776])

def sample(z, tau=1.0, top_k=None, top_p=None, rng=np.random.default_rng(0)):
    p = np.exp(z / tau - (z / tau).max())
    p /= p.sum()
    order = np.argsort(-p)
    keep = np.ones(len(p), dtype=bool)
    if top_k is not None:
        keep[order[top_k:]] = False
    if top_p is not None:
        cutoff = np.searchsorted(np.cumsum(p[order]), top_p) + 1
        keep[order[cutoff:]] = False
    p = np.where(keep, p, 0)
    return vocab[rng.choice(len(p), p=p / p.sum())]

print(sample(z, tau=0.5, top_p=0.9))   # always "on"
```

## Common questions

**What is temperature 0?** Dividing by 0 is not possible, so temperature 0 is defined as the limit: always take the most likely token, which is greedy decoding.

**Does temperature change which token is most likely?** No. It keeps the order of the tokens and only changes how far apart their probabilities are. "on" is first at 0.5, 1 and 2, with 0.957, 0.741 and 0.431.

**Should I use top-k or top-p?** Top-p adapts to the model's confidence. When one token has 0.957, top-p 0.9 keeps just that token, and when the probabilities are spread out it keeps many. Top-k keeps the same number either way. They are often used together.

**Why does the same prompt give different answers?** The token is drawn at random from the kept probabilities. With top-3 here, "on" is drawn 78.3% of the time, "sat" 14.8% and "dog" 7.0%. One different draw changes everything after it.
