# 3.19 · XT3 · 0. Setup

> **Goal:** Learning from a differential equation, the history and the roadmap.
> **Stage:** Camp, Advanced preparation.

## The problem

Science and engineering describe many systems by how they change: heat spreading through a metal plate, air flowing over a wing, a drug clearing from the blood. These descriptions are **differential equations**, and solving them is how engineers predict what a system will do.

Classical solvers handle this well, but each new shape or setting needs a new run. For example, a drug that clears at a rate $k$ follows $dy/dt = -k\,y$, and $k$ differs from patient to patient. A solver gives the curve for one value of $k$. A different $k$ means starting again.

Classical solvers also do not easily combine the equation with measured data. Scientific machine learning trains neural networks with the equation itself built into the loss, so a network can learn from physics, from data, or from both.

## History

| Year | Method | What changed |
|---|---|---|
| 1768 | Euler's method | Solving an equation step by step from a starting value |
| 1950s to 1960s | Finite element method | Solving on a mesh over complex shapes. Still the engineering standard |
| 1998 | Lagaris et al. | A neural network as the solution of a differential equation |
| 2019 | Physics-informed neural networks (Raissi et al.) | The equation as a loss term, computed with automatic differentiation |
| 2020 | Fourier Neural Operator (Li et al.) | Learning a whole family of solutions, so a new case takes one pass |
| 2023 | GraphCast (Lam et al.) | A learned weather model beats the leading physics-based forecast on 90% of the targets tested |

The 1998 idea became practical at scale once deep learning libraries made automatic differentiation and GPUs easy to use.

## The intuition

### The equation as the teacher

A classifier learns from labeled examples. Here the equation does the teaching: wherever the network's output breaks the equation, the loss penalizes it. For example, an equation saying "the rate of change equals minus the value" asks for a slope of −0.5 wherever the value is 0.5, so a function whose value at some moment is 0.5 while its slope there is −0.2 is breaking it.

### An adjustable function

The network is a smooth function of time, or of position and time, with weights that can be adjusted. Training adjusts them until the equation holds at many chosen points.

### Data where it exists

Measurements, when there are some, go into the same loss as an extra term. Unknown constants in the equation, such as a decay rate, can then be learned along with the solution.
